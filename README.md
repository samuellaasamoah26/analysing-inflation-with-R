QUESTION 1: ANALYSIS OF GHANA'S HEADLINE INFLATION TRENDS
Research question: How has headline inflation moved since 1999, and when were the peaks and troughs?
1. Introduction
This analysis examines the movement of Ghana's headline inflation from January 1999 to January 2026 using monthly year-on-year inflation data obtained from the Ghana Statistical Service StatsBank. Headline inflation is represented by the All products category, which captures the overall rate of inflation across the products and services covered by the Consumer Price Index (CPI).
2. Findings and Discussion
The analysis reveals that Ghana's headline inflation has experienced considerable fluctuations over the period under review.
The highest headline inflation rate recorded was 63.0% in March 2001, representing the peak of the series. In contrast, the lowest rate was 0.4% in May 1999, representing the trough.
The overall mean headline inflation rate was approximately 17.31%, while the median was 13.5%. The mean being higher than the median suggests that some periods of exceptionally high inflation raised the overall average.
Following the peak in 2001, inflation fluctuated over the subsequent years. Another significant inflationary period occurred in the early 2020s. The annual average headline inflation rate reached 40.3% in 2023. However, inflation subsequently declined, falling from 23.5% in January 2025 to 3.8% in January 2026.
These movements demonstrate that Ghana has experienced periods of both relatively low inflation and substantial increases in the general price level.
3. Conclusion
The findings indicate that Ghana's headline inflation has not followed a constant pattern since 1999. The series recorded its highest rate in March 2001 and its lowest rate in May 1999, with significant fluctuations occurring throughout the period.
Although inflation rose sharply in the early 2020s, the decline towards January 2026 suggests a moderation in the rate at which consumer prices were increasing.
It is important to note that a decline in the inflation rate does not necessarily mean that prices have fallen. Rather, it indicates that prices are increasing at a slower rate.

QUESTION 2: PRODUCT CATEGORIES WITH THE HIGHEST INFLATION
Research question: Which product categories have had the highest inflation over the long run and over the last five years?
1. Introduction
This analysis examines the product categories that recorded the highest average year-on-year inflation rates in Ghana from January 1999 to January 2026. It also examines the most recent five-year period, February 2021 to January 2026.
The analysis considers the 13 detailed product categories. The broad aggregates All products, Food and Non-food were excluded to avoid double-counting.
2. Long-run Inflation Patterns (January 1999–January 2026)
The five categories with the highest average inflation rates over the full period were:
Rank	Product category	Mean inflation
1	Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels	23.2%
2	Transport	22.2%
3	Recreation, sport and culture	20.2%
4	Alcoholic beverages, tobacco and narcotics	18.6%
5	Furnishings, household equipment and routine household maintenance	18.2%
Each category had 325 monthly observations.
Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels recorded the highest long-run average inflation rate of 23.2%, followed by Transport at 22.2%. This indicates that housing-related expenses and transport experienced relatively high inflation over the period.
3. Inflation Patterns Over the Last Five Years (February 2021–January 2026)
The five categories with the highest average inflation rates during the recent period were:
Rank	Product category	Mean inflation
1	Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels	31.5%
2	Food and non-alcoholic beverages	26.9%
3	Furnishings, household equipment and routine household maintenance	25.6%
4	Personal care, social protection and miscellaneous goods and services	25.4%
5	Alcoholic beverages, tobacco and narcotics	24.5%
Each category had 60 monthly observations.
Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels remained the category with the highest average inflation, recording 31.5%. Food and non-alcoholic beverages ranked second at 26.9%. Furnishings and household equipment, personal care and miscellaneous goods, and alcoholic beverages, tobacco and narcotics also recorded relatively high average inflation rates.
4. Discussion
The results show that housing and related utilities consistently recorded the highest average inflation in both periods.
However, the rankings changed over time. Transport and Recreation, sport and culture were among the five highest categories over the long run but did not appear among the five highest in the recent period. Food and non-alcoholic beverages and Personal care, social protection and miscellaneous goods and services appeared among the top five in the recent period.
This suggests that the pattern of inflation across product categories has changed over time, with food and other household-related expenses becoming particularly prominent in the recent five-year period.
5. Conclusion
The analysis identifies Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels as the category with the highest average inflation over both the long-run and recent periods. Transport ranked second over the full period, while Food and non-alcoholic beverages ranked second over the last five years.
Overall, the findings demonstrate that inflation has affected different product categories at varying rates and that the categories experiencing the highest average inflation have changed over time.
Methodological note: These rankings are based on average category inflation rates. They do not, by themselves, measure each category's contribution to headline inflation, which would also require expenditure weights.
Source: Ghana Statistical Service StatsBank, Consumer Price Index data; author's analysis using R.

 QUESTION 3: PRODUCT CATEGORIES WITH THE HIGHEST AND LOWEST INFLATION IN THE LATEST MONTH
Research question: Which categories are driving prices up or down in the latest month?
1. Introduction
This analysis identifies the product categories recording the highest and lowest year-on-year inflation rates in the latest month available in the dataset, January 2026. The analysis considers the 13 detailed product categories and ranks them according to their inflation rates.
2. Findings and Discussion
The results indicate that Recreation, sport and culture recorded the highest inflation rate at 10.7%, followed by Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels at 9.3%. Insurance and financial services ranked third at 8.0%, while Restaurants and accommodation services recorded 5.5% and Health recorded 4.9%.
On the other hand, Transport recorded the lowest inflation rate at -5.9%. This negative rate indicates that prices in the transport category were lower than in the corresponding month of the previous year.
Other categories with relatively low inflation rates included Alcoholic beverages, tobacco and narcotics and Information and communication, both recording 2.4%. Food and non-alcoholic beverages recorded 3.9%, while Education services recorded 4.1%.
3. Conclusion
The findings show that inflationary pressures in January 2026 were particularly high in Recreation, sport and culture, Housing and related utilities, and Insurance and financial services.
Transport was the only category among the five lowest-ranked categories to record negative inflation, indicating a year-on-year decline in its price level.
Overall, the results demonstrate that inflation varied considerably across product categories during the latest month.
Methodological note: These rankings are based on category-specific year-on-year inflation rates. They identify categories with relatively high or low rates but do not establish their weighted contributions to overall headline inflation.
Source: Ghana Statistical Service StatsBank, Consumer Price Index data; author's analysis using R.
QUESTION 4: COMPARISON OF LOCAL AND IMPORTED INFLATION
Research question: Since 2022, has local or imported inflation been higher?
1. Introduction
This analysis compares Ghana's year-on-year inflation rates for locally produced and imported goods from January 2022 to January 2026. The comparison uses 49 monthly observations obtained from the Ghana Statistical Service Consumer Price Index data.
A difference variable was calculated by subtracting imported inflation from local inflation. A positive difference indicates that local inflation was higher, while a negative difference indicates that imported inflation was higher.
2. Findings and Discussion
The results show that local inflation was higher than imported inflation in 31 out of the 49 months examined. Imported inflation was higher in the remaining 18 months.
The average local inflation rate over the period was approximately 26.95%, compared with 26.59% for imported inflation. The average difference between the two rates was approximately 0.36 percentage points, indicating that local inflation was slightly higher on average.
Local inflation reached its highest rate of 51.9% in December 2022. Imported inflation, however, recorded a higher peak of 62.4% in January 2023.
By January 2026, both inflation rates had declined considerably. Local inflation stood at 4.5%, while imported inflation stood at 2.0%.
The line graph illustrates the monthly movements of local and imported inflation over the period. It shows that the two series fluctuated over time, with their relative positions changing across different months.
3. Conclusion
The findings indicate that local inflation was higher in more months than imported inflation between January 2022 and January 2026. Although imported inflation reached a higher peak during the period, the average inflation rate for locally produced goods was slightly higher.
Overall, the results suggest that inflationary pressures affected both locally produced and imported goods, although their relative movements varied over time.

QUESTION 5: COMPARISON OF GHANA'S INFLATION ACROSS DECADES
Research question: How do the 2000s, 2010s and 2020s compare?
1. Introduction
This analysis compares Ghana's headline inflation across three periods: the 2000s, 2010s and 2020s. Monthly year-on-year inflation data obtained from the Ghana Statistical Service Consumer Price Index were used.
The comparison considers the mean, median, minimum and maximum inflation rates, as well as the percentage of months in which inflation remained below 10%. The 2020s analysis covers January 2020 to January 2026. The 12 available observations for 1999 are presented separately for additional context.
2. Findings and Discussion
The results reveal considerable differences in inflation patterns across the periods.
Period	Observations	Mean inflation	Median inflation	Minimum	Maximum	Months below 10%
1999 only	12	4.95%	4.75%	0.4%	11.3%	91.67%
2000s	120	21.47%	17.20%	3.9%	63.0%	5.83%
2010s	120	11.98%	11.70%	5.8%	19.2%	36.67%
2020s*	73	21.27%	20.40%	3.8%	54.1%	20.55%
The 2000s recorded an average inflation rate of approximately 21.47%, with a median of 17.20%. The highest monthly rate was 63.0%, while the lowest was 3.9%. Only 5.83% of the months recorded inflation below 10%, indicating that inflation was relatively high for much of the decade.
During the 2010s, the average inflation rate declined to approximately 11.98%, while the median was 11.70%. Inflation ranged from a minimum of 5.8% to a maximum of 19.2%. Approximately 36.67% of the months recorded inflation below 10%, suggesting a comparatively more moderate inflation environment.
For the 2020s, up to January 2026, the average inflation rate was approximately 21.27%, with a median of 20.40%. The minimum recorded rate was 3.8%, while the maximum was 54.1%. Approximately 20.55% of the months recorded inflation below 10%.
For additional context, the 12 observations available for 1999 recorded an average inflation rate of 4.95%, with approximately 91.67% of the months below 10%.
3. Comparison of the Periods
The findings show that the 2000s and 2020s experienced considerably higher average inflation than the 2010s. The 2010s recorded the lowest average inflation among the three periods.
Although the 2020s recorded an average inflation rate similar to that of the 2000s, the 2000s had a slightly higher average. The 2000s also had fewer months with inflation below 10%.
The 2010s, in contrast, experienced a relatively higher proportion of months with inflation below 10%, suggesting a comparatively more moderate inflation environment.
4. Conclusion
The analysis demonstrates that Ghana experienced different inflation conditions across the three periods. The 2000s and 2020s were characterised by relatively high average inflation, whereas the 2010s recorded a substantially lower average rate.
Overall, the findings indicate that Ghana's inflation performance has varied considerably over time, with the 2010s representing a period of comparatively lower inflation between two periods of higher inflation.
